How to price pet portrait commissions (a simple per-square-inch method)

By Kimberly Marnell, founder · November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Pricing is the question artists ask most, and the one buyers care about most. A per-square-inch rate is one of the simplest ways to set prices that are fair, consistent and easy to explain. Here's how to build one.

Step 1: choose a rate per square inch

Multiply width by height to get square inches, then multiply by your rate. At $2.50 per square inch:

SizeSquare inchesPrice at $2.50
8 × 10 in80$200
11 × 14 in154$385
16 × 20 in320$800
18 × 24 in432$1,080

$2.50 is an example, not a recommendation. Your rate depends on your medium, detail, experience and demand.

Step 2: check it against your time

A rate only works if it pays you. Estimate your hours for a typical size, including sketching, painting, photos, messages and packing. Divide the price by those hours. If the result is less than you want to earn per hour, raise the rate or offer fewer sizes. Remember to subtract materials, shipping supplies and any platform fee first.

Step 3: add a few clear extras

  • Extra pet or person. A percentage of the base price (many artists use 20–50%) is easier to explain than a flat amount, because it scales with size.
  • Detailed background. A plain or softly blended background can be included; a full scene is an add-on.
  • Rush date. If a client needs it sooner than your usual timeline, charge for moving other work.

Step 4: think about very large sizes

A flat rate grows fast. At $2.50, a 24 × 36 in painting is $2,160. Some painters lower the per-inch rate a little for big canvases, because the time doesn't grow as quickly as the surface. Others keep large sizes by request only.

Step 5: publish a starting price

Put "From $200" (or your smallest size) where buyers can see it. A starting price answers the first question every buyer has and brings you inquiries from people who can afford your work.

When to raise prices

Good signs: you're booked several weeks out, clients rarely question the price, or you're turning work away. Raise prices for new orders and honor quotes you've already sent.
