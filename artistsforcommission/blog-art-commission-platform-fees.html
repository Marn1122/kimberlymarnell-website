Art commission platform fees compared: what you keep on a $500 commission

By Kimberly Marnell, founder · November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Every platform takes something. The question is how much, when, and whether the fee is one number or several stacked together. Here is what an artist in the US keeps on a single $500 commission on four common options, using each platform's published fees as of November 2026.

The short version

PlatformWhat's takenYou keep on $500
Artists for Commission10%, card processing included$450.00
VGen5% service fee + 2.9% + $0.30 processing$460.20
Etsy6.5% transaction + 3% + $0.25 processing + $0.20 listing$452.05
Etsy, sale from an Offsite AdThe above + 15% Offsite Ads fee$377.05
Fiverr20% service fee$400.00

US rates, one $500 commission, no shipping, tax or currency conversion. Fees change, so check each platform's help pages before you decide. Etsy's Offsite Ads fee is 12% for sellers over $10,000 a year.

What the numbers don't show

On fees alone, VGen and a standard Etsy sale come out slightly ahead of us on a $500 order, and Fiverr is the most expensive. We'd rather say that plainly. Here is what else is worth weighing:

  • One fee, no surprises. Our 10% includes card processing. There's no listing fee, no ad fee and no monthly plan.
  • Who the buyers are. VGen is built largely around digital art and creator clients. Etsy is a huge general marketplace where commissions compete with everything else. Fiverr is a freelance services site. Artists for Commission is only for painted portraits of pets, people and homes from photos.
  • Ads you don't control. On Etsy, a sale that came through an Offsite Ad costs an extra 15% (12% for larger shops). You don't choose which sales that applies to.
  • You're not locked in. Joining us doesn't stop you selling anywhere else. We only ask that clients who find you here order through the site.

How the 10% is taken

Once you connect your payouts, each payment a client makes through the site is split automatically: 90% goes to you and 10% to the platform. You don't invoice us or pay anything later, and deposits and final payments are split the same way.

Bottom line

If you already have steady buyers on another platform, keep them. If you paint portraits from photos and want a place built for exactly that, with one simple fee, we'd love to see your work.
