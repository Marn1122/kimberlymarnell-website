What to put in your art commission terms: deposits, revisions and timelines

By Kimberly Marnell, founder · November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Clear terms prevent most commission problems before they start. They don't need to be long or written in legal language. They need to answer the questions a client will eventually ask. Use this checklist to write your own.

Payment

  • How much is due to start (for example, 50%) and when the rest is due (for example, before shipping).
  • Whether small pieces are paid in full up front.
  • When the deposit becomes non-refundable, usually once you've started work.

Photos and details

  • What photos you need, and that the client must have the right to use them.
  • Which details you'll follow closely (markings, eye color) and where you'll use artistic judgment (background, lighting).

Revisions

  • When the client can request changes. A sketch stage is the best time.
  • How many rounds of changes are included, and what further changes cost.
  • What counts as a revision (adjusting a feature) versus a new commission (changing the subject or composition).

Timeline

  • Your usual turnaround from receiving photos and deposit.
  • Rush pricing, if you offer it, and that holiday dates fill early.
  • What happens if the client is slow to reply at an approval stage.

Shipping and delivery

  • How the painting is packed, how it ships and who pays.
  • Drying time for oils before shipping, if it applies.
  • Whether shipping is insured and what happens if a piece arrives damaged.

Cancellations and refunds

  • What's refunded if the client cancels before you start, after the sketch, or after painting begins.
  • What happens if you can't complete the commission.

Copyright and sharing

  • The client owns the physical painting; you keep the copyright unless you agree otherwise in writing.
  • Whether you may share the finished piece online, and how a client can ask you not to.
  • That prints, merchandise or commercial use need a separate agreement.

Keep it friendly

Write your terms in the same voice you use with clients. Link to them from your order form, and ask clients to confirm they've read them before paying the deposit. Good terms read less like a contract and more like a promise about how you work.
