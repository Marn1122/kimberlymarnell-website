How to get art commissions: 9 practical steps for painters

By Kimberly Marnell, founder · November 22, 2026 · 7 min read

Most people who want a custom painting aren't browsing galleries. They have a photo of a dog, a parent or a first home, and they want it painted well, at a price they understand, without a lot of back and forth. If you can make that easy, you can get commissions. Here is how.

1. Pick a few things you're known for

"I paint anything" is hard to buy. "Pet portraits in oil, from your photo" is easy. Choose one to three subjects you enjoy and can repeat with confidence: pets, people and families, or houses and places. You can always add more later, but a clear offer is what makes a stranger trust you with their photo.

2. Show finished commissions, not just personal work

Clients want to see what they'll get. The strongest portfolio piece is a finished commission shown next to the client's original photo. Six to eight of those will do more than thirty personal pieces. If you don't have commissions yet, paint two or three for friends or family at cost, with permission to show them.

3. Publish prices

A visible starting price removes the biggest worry a buyer has: "Can I afford this?" A simple per-square-inch rate, or a short list of sizes with prices, works well. Put add-ons in writing too: an extra pet or person, a detailed background, a rush date. Our pricing guide walks through the math.

4. Make ordering one clear path

Every extra message is a chance for the client to drift away. Decide what you need up front (the photo, the size, the subject count, the deadline) and ask for it in one form. Then tell them exactly what happens next and when.

5. Take a deposit before you start

A deposit protects your time and confirms the client is serious. Many painters ask for half up front and the rest before shipping, and take full payment for smaller pieces. Whatever you choose, put it in your terms. See what to put in your commission terms.

6. Ask for good photos

Your painting can only be as good as the reference. Tell clients what works: natural light, eye level, sharp focus on the eyes, and the whole subject in frame. A short photo guide saves you hours of guessing. Ours is here if you want an example.

7. Share progress

A sketch for approval and one in-progress photo make clients feel involved, cut down on revisions and give you content to post. Agree in advance how many changes are included at each stage.

8. Be where buyers already look

Commission buyers often start with a search like "custom pet portrait from photo" or "paint my house." Being listed on a site built for those searches puts you in front of people who are ready to order. Your own social posts help too, especially finished pieces with the client's story (with their permission).

9. Turn every client into the next one

Ship with a short thank-you note, ask for a photo of the painting on their wall, and ask if you may share it. Many commissions are gifts, so happy clients often come back for the next birthday or holiday, and send friends.
