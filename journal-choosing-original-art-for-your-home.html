Choosing original art for your home.

The right size, the right height and a little care: a short, practical guide to living with original art, with examples from Kimberly's available paintings.

By Kimberly Marnell · Haven Art House

Measure the wall, then the furniture

The most common mistake is choosing a painting that is too small for its wall. Over a sofa, bed or console, a painting (or a group) usually looks best at about two-thirds of the furniture's width. Over a 7-foot sofa, that is roughly 56 inches across.

A trick that saves guessing: cut paper or painter's tape to the size of the painting and put it on the wall for a day. You'll know quickly whether it fits.

Hang it at eye level

Galleries hang work so that its center sits about 57 to 60 inches from the floor. Over furniture, leave 6 to 10 inches between the top of the furniture and the bottom of the frame so the two read as one group.

Sizes from Kimberly's available work

Statement pieces: living rooms, above a sofa or bed

Medium works: entries, bedrooms, offices

At 24 × 18 inches, paintings like Queen of the Night, Golden Hour and the charcoal My Body work well on a single wall, beside a doorway, or as a pair.

Small works: shelves, reading corners, gallery walls

Smaller pieces such as Comfy (11 × 8 in), Flourish (14 × 11 in) and Look Me in the Eye (7 × 15 in) reward close looking. Hang them where people pass slowly, or group several with even spacing of 2 to 3 inches.

Caring for works on paper

Watercolor, charcoal and ink on paper should be framed under glass, ideally UV-filtering glass, with a mat so the surface doesn't touch the glazing. Keep any original out of direct sunlight and away from bathrooms and kitchens where humidity swings. Oil on canvas is sturdier, but sun still fades it over time.
