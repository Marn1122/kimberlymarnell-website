Journal · Studio notes

Charcoal, skin and fabric.

Two charcoal drawings, My Body and Comfy, show the side of Kimberly's work that has no color to hide behind.

By Kimberly Marnell · Haven Art House

Charcoal is unforgiving. There is no color to carry a weak passage, so everything depends on drawing and on value: how light turns into shadow across a form. In these two pieces, Kimberly lets the figure fill the page and lets the light do the work.

My Body

A figure folds into itself, a hand gripping at the top of the sheet, the back and shoulder pressed toward the viewer. There is no face and no setting, only skin, muscle and the creases where the body bends, pulled out of a dark, grainy ground.

Stand close and look at the edges: some dissolve into the dark, others are sharp. That play between hard and soft edges is what makes the form feel heavy and real on a flat sheet of cotton paper.

Comfy

A smaller, quieter drawing: crossed bare feet, loose folds of fabric and two hands resting at the ankles. The pose is ordinary, the kind of moment nobody would think to photograph. Kimberly treats it with the same attention she gives a full figure, from the knuckles to the weight of cloth falling over the knee.

At 11 × 8 inches, Comfy is made for close viewing: a bedside wall, a reading nook, a shelf.

Framing charcoal

Charcoal sits on the surface of the paper, so it should always be framed under glass with a mat or spacers to keep the drawing from touching the glazing. Frame it once and it will last.
